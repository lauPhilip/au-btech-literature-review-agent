# Redesign: one calm frame for every page

The redesign gives TraceableAI the simplicity of its sibling Coursebot: one calm landing screen, a fixed app frame with a header and footer that never move, and one task per page. It also brings a new mark, the "reader bot", and a light and a dark theme. The full brief, with every page described at every width, is Lau's redesign handover of 20 November 2026; the brand files and their rules are in `AuBtechReviewAgent/wwwroot/brand/` (`BRAND.md`).

Lau settled two questions before the build started. Cards have a 16 px radius and buttons and fields a 10 px radius, which replaces the old rule that only buttons are rounded. Claude pushes the branches and merges the pull requests once all checks pass, as for every other feature.

## The order of the work

The redesign is built as a series of small pull requests, so the site stays usable after each merge and every step can be checked with screenshots. A page keeps its old look until its own pull request lands; the shared tokens come first so that later pages only use them.

|---|---|---|

## Rules that hold throughout

The Content-Security-Policy stays as strict as it is. Fonts are served from `wwwroot/fonts`, and the script that sets the theme before the first paint is a file in `wwwroot/js`, not an inline script. Pages move to the theme-aware colours (`page`, `soft`, `ink`, `muted`, `line`, `primary` and the five verdicts) when their own pull request comes; until then the fixed `brand` navy keeps them looking as before, so dark mode is switched on only once the frame and the pages are ready for it. The print styles keep working, so a report still prints without the frame.

From step 3 the frame exists before the pages use it. The two layouts could be seen on the development pages `/design/frame` and `/design/landing-frame` (the second was removed in step 5, when the landing page took its place), and the CI accessibility job checks them in both themes and uploads screenshots of every checked page at 390, 1440 and 1920 px as the workflow artifact `a11y-screenshots`. That artifact replaces screenshots of the live site, which no longer show the markup of a pull request. From step 4 every page sits in a layout. From step 5 the landing page uses the theme-aware colours and shows the theme toggle; Lau chose to offer it on the landing page only. The app pages stay light until step 12: `ThemeToggle.ReadyFor` marks only the landing page (and the `/design` pages) as ready for the dark theme, `theme.js` keeps every other page light, and `AppLayout` shows the toggle only once `ThemeToggle.OfferedOnAppPages` is on. A visitor who has not chosen a theme gets the light one until step 12 also switches on `ThemeToggle.FollowSystem`. The CI accessibility job checks the landing page in both themes. From step 6 New review opens on the composer and the criteria panel (`ReviewComposer`, `CriteriaPanel`); they use the theme-aware colours but stay light like the other app pages, and the run-plan preview they replaced (`RunPlanPreview`) is gone. From step 7 a run is shown in the same column: `RunProgressPanel` while it is going, waiting or stopped, with the seven steps mapped from the run's plan by `ReviewSteps`, and `RunSummaryCard` when it is done; the older two-column view is gone. A real run cannot be started in CI, so `/design/run` (development only) shows the three states with sample figures for the accessibility job. The brief's "Cancel review" is left out, because the engine cannot stop a run once it has started. From step 8 Review output is one long report in the frame: a toolbar, one report card with the citation summary and every section in order at a reading width of 70 characters, a contents list from 1024 px (`ReportContents`) and a citation-check panel from 1500 px (`CitationCheckPanel`). `/design/report` (development only) shows it with a made-up run (`SampleReport`) for the accessibility job. "← All reviews" became "Back to the run", since there is no list of reviews, and section 3 keeps its figures without the brief's study table, because the study tables stay in the appendix (issue #139). From step 9 Metrics shows every run as one stacked bar labelled with its short run id, then the verdict shares per run, the run data and the groups by version, each with a totals row; `/design/metrics` (development only) shows it with made-up runs (`SampleMetrics`). Lau chose to leave out the old per-run detail ("Where the citations fail", cost by stage) for now. From step 10 the five trust pages share one template (`DocPage`): a side list of the five pages with the current page's sections, and one reading card. Review type shows the reviews you can start as two large cards and the ones to come as a short list, and Not found shows the mark with two ways on.

Three questions stay open and are settled with Lau when their pull request comes: whether Coursebot's own typeface replaces Poppins and Inter, how the multivocal pages look in detail, and whether the phone screens really fit 390 × 844 once they are measured in a browser.
