# Synthesis

Turning the evidence into text. `ReviewEvidence.cs` is the evidence every kind of review writes from, in one shape: the numbered sources with their reference lines, verified findings and text, and the themes with their sources and codes; a module fills it (the systematic review in `Modules/Systematic/SystematicEvidence.cs`) and the writing steps read only it. `ReviewWriter.cs` holds the writing stages every kind of review shares: the grounded outline, one cited subsection per theme with its fill pass, the discussion, the automated peer review with one revision, and the title, abstract, rationale and objectives written last from the checked sections; `WritingProfile` (`WritingProfile.cs`) gives the words a review is named by in the prompts, the ids of its research questions and the names of its closing parts (the systematic review's are the defaults, so its prompts stay as they were, which `Golden/systematic-writing-prompts.txt` in the tests checks word for word). The class is split by stage: the outline, theme subsections and discussion are in `ReviewWriter.Themes.cs`, the peer review in `ReviewWriter.PeerReview.cs` (its critique gets the facts about the evidence base, the sources left out of the themes and each theme's sources, and a theme revision may not cite outside its theme), the closing sections and their checks in `ReviewWriter.Closing.cs`, the answers to the research questions, which the discussion call writes first and the closing sections and the front matter then get as checked text, in `ReviewWriter.Answers.cs`, the rule that a written sentence cites at most three references, ideally one or two, checked in code on every written section (`ReviewWriter.CitationLoad.cs`, with the soft guard the other soft checks use), the shape of a theme subsection (`ReviewWriter.ThemeShape.cs`: a theme of up to five sources cites every one with the fill pass, a larger one is synthesised from its most direct sources, sent back when it reads as a list of one-source summaries, and revised where it repeats a claim of an earlier theme), the guard that keeps a result figure to its theme, the abstract and one place after the results (sending an answer back at most twice, on file) in `ReviewWriter.Figures.cs`, the rule and the code check that keep what the sources found apart from the review's own reading (`EvidenceThenReadingRule` and `UncitedAttribution`: a finding ends with its citation, and an uncited sentence may not report one) in `ReviewWriter.Attribution.cs`, the soft checks that a result carries the figures its verified finding gives and that the discussion reads the results instead of retelling them or reading agreement into them (issue #572) in `ReviewWriter.Readings.cs`, the front matter and practitioner summary in `ReviewWriter.cs` (the front matter gets the sources that are weak evidence on their own, and its rationale may not open on one of them alone as fact: `ReviewWriter.WeakSources.cs`), and the research questions in the prompts (`AimBlock`) and the text about them written in code (the objectives, and the answer to a question no evidence bears on) in `ReviewWriter.Questions.cs`. With research questions the coding sees them and the eligibility criteria, codes what bears on any question and names the questions each code bears on (`ThematicSynthesis.CodingQuestions.cs`), the codebook organises the themes by question and lists the questions no code bears on, and every writing step from there on sees the questions; without them every prompt is as before. `PrismaReviewEngine.Thematic.cs`, `ThematicSynthesis.cs` and `ThematicSynthesis.Codebook.cs` code the verified findings, group them into themes and write one cited subsection per theme, with guards in code so no study and no citation is lost on the way. `ThematicSynthesis.Cache.cs` keeps each validated coding and codebook answer in the review cache under its stage, prompt version, model and whole prompt, so a rerun on the same studies gets the same codes and themes; a reused answer passes the same validator again, its codes are marked `FromCache`, and the methods text counts what was reused (`ReuseSentence`). `GroundingContextBuilder.cs` chooses which excerpts each paper contributes. `ArtifactBuilder.cs` builds the diagram, table or list from the finished text (its shape is in `ReviewArtifact.cs`; the codebook's in `ThematicCodebook.cs`), and `MermaidSanitizer.cs` keeps its diagram code safe. `ArtifactLinks.cs` checks each link of a diagram against the studies both of its boxes cite and marks a link none of them states as the review's own reading, drawn dashed (issue #571); a map keeps at most three dashed links and records the rest as dropped (issue #680). `ReviewWriter.RedThread.cs` makes the themes build on each other: each theme is told which of its sources earlier themes already hold, so it refers to those findings instead of restating them, a broad theme compares how its sources approach the question, and a source that bears on a theme only indirectly may be said to do so or left out with its reason on file (issues #674 and #675). `ReviewWriter.Claims.cs` keeps the front matter and the conclusion within the certainty of the evidence, leaves out counts of zero and asks the rationale for an argument instead of a list, with soft checks in code (issues #675 and #676). `StylisticRefinerUtility.cs` gives the abstract and rationale a language edit into plain technical English (based on ASD-STE100), which `PlainEnglish.cs` checks and measures in code (sentence length, passive voice, noun clusters, paragraph length), and `ProseCleaner.cs` turns any Markdown the model wrote back into plain prose before the citations are checked.
