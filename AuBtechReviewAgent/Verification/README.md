# Verification

Checking that every citation points somewhere real and says what the sentence claims, for every kind of review. `Grounding.cs` is the entry point a module uses: it checks every citation of a text against its numbered sources and repairs the rejected ones once, and it re-checks a single citation on request. `CitationValidator.cs` removes citation numbers that are not in the reference list. `CitationSupportChecker.Check.cs` compares each cited sentence with the cited paper and only accepts a verdict whose quote is found word for word in the paper; weak verdicts get a second, independent check. `CitationSupportChecker.cs` holds the verdicts and the text helpers the check and the pages share (sentence split, markers, the part of a sentence attributed to a paper, the quote match), and `CitationSupportResult.cs` the verdict records and their summary. `CanonicalText.cs` keeps a source's normalised form once, with a map back to the original text, and `QuoteSpan.cs` reads every quote match again in the original text, so a quote across a sentence end or stitched from two passages is not taken as word for word (#606). `PrismaReviewEngine.Recheck.cs` checks a single orange or red citation again after the run. `PrismaReviewEngine.ReportEdit.cs` is the checked editor: the run's owner rewrites one sentence of the finished report, every citation in it is checked again on the fast check (`Grounding.CheckEditAsync`, with the sentence wrapped as data), and the edit is kept with its verdicts; it also undoes an edit. `CitationRepairer.cs` rewrites a sentence with a rejected citation from the source's evidence, drops the citation or deletes the sentence, never adds a citation, and checks the changed sentences again. `CitationRepairer.Roles.cs` names each section's role in the repair prompt ("Future research: proposes a study"), so a reading, proposal or recommendation keeps its role by losing the citation rather than being rewritten into a finding. `CitationRepairer.SecondPass.cs` is the second pass: it rewrites what is still partly supported once more and removes what is still not supported, in code, listing every removed citation. The prompts name the kind of review they serve, so the same check works for a systematic or a multivocal review. `CitationCheckSteps.cs` records the counts after each step (first check, second check, repair, second pass, what is left) as one sequence that adds up, and writes the paragraph of the methods that reports the check (issue #679).
